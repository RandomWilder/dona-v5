## The three changes

**Two tiers.** A qualified label (`כתובת המושכר` / `הנכס` / `הדירה`) is trusted wherever it stands; a
bare label or a street in a sentence is read only when the first found nothing. **The tier decides, not
the position** — every standard form prints its parties above its property clause.

**`(?<![א-ת])` on the loose needle**, so `רחוב` is no longer reachable through `מרחוב` while `ברחוב`
still reads; and a loose match whose own line carries an identity marker before it is skipped for the
next, the reading being null where there is no other. The line, not the paragraph: a wider window
starts refusing property clauses that merely stand near the parties block.

**`דירה מס ' 206-7` is one flat.** The spaced apostrophe defeated the `מס` branch outright — what came
back was null, not `206` — and the hyphen would have cut a real number in half. `foldPlace` already
drops hyphens, so `apartmentMatches` needed no edit; asserted rather than assumed.

## The annex, ruled and not patched

**A12 does not read an annex.** It sits past the pages the online call reads and the byte bound is on
the request carrying the whole file, so no page selection can reach it; a `גוש`/`חלקה` has nothing to
resolve against, the estate being keyed on an address; and the published form defers the same way, so
this is the form and not the specimen. Three nulls, written as a case so nobody later teaches the
reader to guess a flat out of a parcel.
