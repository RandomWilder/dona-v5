# 7.2b — The day the system is having

**Closed 15 Sep 2026.** Kernel + `src/scope/` + three modules. No migration. No new flow: it serves
every flow there is, Q1 and Q2 first.

Every date was derived from an instant in UTC. Israel is UTC+2/+3, so for two or three hours after
midnight the system's today was the country's yesterday. 7.2 met it as a wrong stamp on a schema row.

## The fourteen occurrences, in ten files

**Twelve asked *what day is it now*** and moved to `today(clock)`: the isolation join's
`TENANCY_ACTIVE_TODAY` (**who lives in a unit**); `utcDay` in the obligation state machine, *deleted*
rather than left uncalled; which tenancies expire tonight; Q5 expiring leases and Q3 overdue
inspections; which declaration governed a reading (R18); the 7.2 stamp ×2; the two register CLIs;
`measure-scale.ts` ×2, so the benchmark measures the query the app runs.

**Two were arithmetic on a date-only string** — zone-free and correct — lifted, not changed, into
`addDays` in `src/kernel/clock.ts`: `addUtcDays` in `status.ts` and `shift` in `register/fixtures/`
were the same six lines. A deviation from the todo bullet, which said `addUtcDays` stays put, and
recorded there: guard five carries no exclusion list beyond the kernel file, and lifting the
arithmetic was cheaper than writing two module names into a guard's exemptions.

**`grep` found thirteen and missed one.** `shift` was formatted across two lines; the guard collapses
whitespace, which is how the fourteenth was found — before the fix, not after.

Left alone, each saying why in its own file: `googDate` in `kernel/objects.ts` (GCS V4 signing
*requires* UTC) and the date round-trip in `register/internal/row.ts` (rejecting `2026-02-31`).

The signature change everywhere is `Date` → `Clock`: a caller that can pass an instant can pass the
wrong one, and every call site already held a clock. `ScopeOptions.clock` went with it — nothing set
it, and a second clock beside the real one is a trap.
