Dona Dom tenant service platform · rollout

Sixteen Demos to Pilot

A sixteen-week sequence built backwards from a promise: every Thursday, something real is on screen. Four monthly milestones carry the decisions; the weeks in between carry the proof.

Span 16 weeks · 4 months Cadence Thursday demo, Wednesday freeze Gates M1–M4, one per month Scope admin console 1,500 units · agent 72 units

The premise

A weekly demo is not a weekly feature

Promising a working feature every week guarantees a broken promise around week three, when the work is Meta verification and a data backfill. Promising evidence of progress every week is a promise that survives contact. Three kinds of demo count — and each week's kind is declared on Monday, not discovered on Thursday.

Working software

They click it. A screen that did not exist last week, on the same URL as last week. The most common kind, but not the only currency.

Real data

The same screen as last week, now holding Dona Dom's actual buildings, leases and tenancies. Stakeholders find our 1,500 units more convincing than a new button.

Evidence

A measured number that closes an open question. Per-field OCR accuracy on 40 real leases. A live attempt to breach tenant isolation, and its failure.

MONTH ONE

The record exists, and you can trust it

Weeks 1–4
01Software

A URL, a schema, and a filed application

You show
A live link stakeholders open themselves: buildings and units for Shoham, real names, real addresses. Plus a screenshot of the Meta business verification submission, timestamped.
Underneath
Cloud Run + Cloud SQL in me-west1, auth, the entity spine. Verification is filed on day one because it takes 4–6 weeks and nothing we do makes it faster.
Also this week
Name the pilot owner and agree the three success numbers. Without both, week 12 has no gate to pass.
02Real data

All 1,500 units, from whatever holds the register today

You show
The same screen, now with every building across Shoham, Beit Shemesh, Ashdod, Lod and Ashkelon — units, tenancies, parties. Searchable.
Underneath
Import from the Priority export. The documents are on Google Drive — that much is settled, and it makes the backfill an ingestion problem against a known source rather than an expedition. What is still open is the structured register: whether Priority stays master for units and tenancies, or we do.
If it goes wrong
If the structured data turns out to be a spreadsheet per site rather than one export, that discovery is the demo — and it is the single most valuable finding of the quarter. See Backfill below.
03Software

Documents filed against units

You show
Pull a real lease off the Drive, then find it again in four seconds. Hashed, dated, attached to unit and tenancy, immutable.
Underneath
Cloud Storage, the eight document schemas, the declared-type upload flow, and the Drive ingestion path: files are copied and hashed at ingest, the Drive id kept as provenance and the folder path kept as a hint that pre-fills a binding — never as the binding itself. Drive is a source, never the system of record. Nothing is read yet; this is a filing cabinet with a search box.
Why it lands
It is already a business win over the status quo, before a single line of AI exists. Say that out loud in the room.
04Software

The machine reads a lease — and shows its work

You show
Drop in a real Hebrew lease. Rent, dates, parties and clauses appear as fields. Click any value and the page image scrolls to the pixels it came from, with a confidence score.
Underneath
Document AI general OCR for coordinates and confidence; OpenAI for mapping into the declared schema. Two engines, because a language model asked for coordinates invents plausible ones.
Evidence
Per-field accuracy across ~40 real leases, on a slide. This is the number that decides how much human review the backfill needs.
Milestone M1

There is a system of record for 1,500 units, and every value in it can be traced to the paper it came from.

MONTH TWO

The rules are explicit, and the console works without AI

Weeks 5–8
05Real data

Paper becomes truth

You show
An amendment arrives for a real unit. The tenancy updates. A change log records old → new, who approved it, and which document caused it. Then a tenancy ends because a date passed, with no document at all.
Underneath
Promotion with provenance, tenancy reconciliation, the append-only event log.
Closes
How many distinct terms profiles are actually in force. The answer sizes week 6.
06Software

Who pays for this, and why

You show
Pick a fault category and a unit. Get an answer — tenant, operator, or contractor warranty — with the clause and the policy version behind it. Then edit the table live in front of them and watch the answer change.
Underneath
The responsibility matrix as versioned, admin-editable data, with an asset_in_warranty dimension fed by the asset register that week 3's handover protocols seed. Rules supersede by date and never overwrite, and every resolved call snapshots the rule version that decided it. No AI anywhere near this decision — it has to be defensible in a dispute a year from now, and a dispute only ever asks about the past.
Why it lands
It is their own business rules on screen, in their own vocabulary, including תקופת הבדק. This is usually the demo where a stakeholder starts correcting the table — which is exactly what you want.
07Software

A ticket, start to finish, by hand

You show
Open a service call in the console and walk it through the canonical states by hand — NEW · IDENTIFIED · TRIAGED · RESPONSIBILITY SET · WINDOWS COLLECTED · OFFERED · SCHEDULED · CLOSED — plus the three exits. Watch the SLA clock run and the escalation fire. No WhatsApp, no agent: a human doing what the agent will later do.
Underneath
State machine, SLA policies, timers, escalation rules — and the emergency bypass, which is a policy row and a routing rule rather than a priority label. It has to be live and tested here, in month two, because it must exist before the agent takes its first real tenant message in week 10. The async negotiation engine starts here and runs underneath for the next six weeks.
08Evidence

Try to break tenant isolation, live

You show
The hard constraint, tested in the room. Query as one tenant's phone number and attempt to reach another tenant's documents, unit, or history — through the console, through the API, and by asking the model to do it. Every path returns nothing.
Underneath
Scoping enforced in SQL before any model call. The scope is a view, not a column — a misbehaving model cannot widen a scope it never held.
Milestone M2

The operator console is usable on its own. If the agent were cancelled tomorrow, this is still a product worth having — and that is your negotiating position for the rest of the project.

MONTH THREE

The agent takes the call and books the visit

Weeks 9–12
09Software

Message the number from your own phone

You show
Hand a stakeholder the WhatsApp number. They message it. It replies with their name, their unit and their tenancy — after a one-time code delivered through WhatsApp itself.
Underneath
Cloud API webhooks both directions, phone → party binding, conversation and message tables.
Depends on
Meta verification, filed week 1. Eight weeks of runway against a 4–6 week process — the buffer is deliberate.
10Software

"Who fixes my dripping tap?"

You show
A tenant describes a fault in plain Hebrew. The agent triages it, answers from the tenant's own lease and the knowledge base, and either resolves it as self-service or opens a ticket.
Underneath
Tenant-facing agent with scoped tools only. It reads the responsibility matrix; it does not decide responsibility. No prices, ever.
11Software

Both sides of the switchboard

You show
The full loop on two phones on the table. Tenant reports a fault; the agent collects windows; the agent WhatsApps a provider with the address and slot options; the provider counter-proposes; the tenant accepts; the visit is booked. Neither human ever sees an app.
Underneath
The hard part, finally surfacing: WINDOWS COLLECTED → OFFERED is two-sided asynchronous negotiation — two people never online at once, no delivery guarantee, either side free to go silent. Roughly seventy percent of the engineering lives between those two states.
12Evidence

Live, with real tenants and real tradesmen

You show
The 72-unit building is on the agent. Real calls from real tenants, real providers, a week of history. Every number measured against the three agreed in week 1.
Underneath
Escalation queue staffed by the pilot owner, watching daily.
Milestone M3 · go / no-go

The loop is closed end to end on one building. The decision at this gate is not "does it work" — it is "do the three numbers justify month four."

MONTH FOUR

It survives contact with reality

Weeks 13–16
13Evidence

What happens when the plumber goes quiet

You show
A deliberate chaos run. A provider ghosts for 36 hours; a tenant stops replying mid-thread; a message fails to deliver. Show the timeouts firing, the retries, the fallback provider, and the escalation reaching a human at the right moment.
Underneath
The unglamorous three-quarters of the negotiation engine. Announce this as an evidence week on Monday — there is no new screen, and that is fine.
14Software

Voice notes and five languages

You show
A tradesman replies with a 20-second voice note; the agent understands it and the console shows both the original and the Hebrew. A Russian-speaking tenant is served in Russian while the provider side stays Hebrew throughout.
Underneath
Transcription, original + canonical Hebrew message bodies, and the separation of service traffic from marketing traffic required by חוק התקשורת.
15Real data

The pilot dashboard, with four weeks of history

You show
Resolution rate, time-to-booked, escalation rate, self-service share, cost per call — from actual pilot traffic, not projections. Plus the audit trail behind any single ticket.
Underneath
Build this early and cheaply; by week 15 it fills itself. It is both a demo in its own right and the evidence base for M4.
16Evidence

Pilot review and the scale decision

You show
One month of operation on 72 units, measured. What the agent handled alone, what it escalated and why, what it cost, and what breaks first at 200 units and at 1,500.
The ask
Which building goes second, and on what date.
Milestone M4

A scale-out decision made on measured data rather than on confidence — with sixteen weeks of demos behind it that nobody has to take on faith.

The ritual

How to run the twenty minutes

The cadence is worth as much as the code. These are the rules that keep it credible when a week goes badly — which some week will.

01

Declare the demo type on Monday

Announce in advance whether this week delivers software, data, or evidence. Expectation set on Monday is a plan; expectation set on Thursday is an excuse.

02

Freeze Wednesday, demo Thursday

Never demo something finished that morning. Thursday also leaves Friday for the follow-up asks, so the week does not end on a cliff.

03

Same link, same ticket, every week

Stakeholders should watch one service call get progressively more real, not meet a new toy each week. Continuity is what makes progress legible.

04

Three slides, and one of them is asks

What moved · what it cost · what I need from you. The standing asks slide is how the open questions in section 07 of the brief get owners at Dona Dom — the demo is your decision-extraction mechanism.

05

Show the ugly weeks too

Week 13 has no new screen. Demo the chaos test instead. A team that only demos wins is a team nobody believes in month five.

06

Re-plan in public at each M

Priorities will change and requirements will appear. The monthly gate is where they get absorbed on the record, not silently between weeks.

Honest exposure

What can break this, and when

Each of these is pre-announced at the week-1 demo. A risk described in advance is a plan; the same risk described afterwards is a failure.

Backfill — the structured register may not exist in usable form

Bites week 2

The document side is answered: lease and building paperwork is organised per apartment on Google Drive, so weeks 3–4 are ingestion against a known source. The exposure moved to the structured register. If the 1,500 units live in a clean Priority export, weeks 1–3 hold. If they live in a spreadsheet per site, or exist only as the leases themselves, backfill becomes its own workstream on Dona Dom's calendar — and the real critical path, worse than Meta verification.

Fallback: Act I continues against a curated slice of the 72 pilot units. Month one demos still land; the 1,500-unit claim moves to M2 and a named Dona Dom owner takes the backfill. Nothing downstream slips.

Meta business verification may stall

Bites week 9

Four to six weeks is the normal case, and filing in week 1 buys an eight-week runway. Rejections over entity documents or number ownership can consume that.

Fallback: build weeks 9–11 against a message simulator and demo the loop with the transport stubbed, then swap in the live number. A BSP stays in reserve as the escape hatch. Verification is also a standing line on the asks slide from week 1 — visible to management as their dependency, not yours.

Demo pressure distorts what gets built

Bites weeks 11–13

The visible work crowds out the async negotiation engine, which is 70% of the real effort and photographs badly. This is the failure mode of every demo-driven programme.

Fallback: one declared evidence week per month, budgeted up front, plus a standing "what's underneath" line in every demo so the invisible work is never invisible. The engine gets a six-week band on the plan precisely so it is not squeezed into one.

The cadence itself costs about half a day a week

Ongoing

Preparation, delivery and follow-ups run roughly 6–8% of engineering capacity across sixteen weeks. It is worth paying — but it belongs in the plan, not absorbed silently and then blamed for a slip in month three.

One structural note

The most valuable thing in this plan is M2. By the end of month two you hold an operator console covering all 1,500 units, with traceable documents and explicit business rules — and none of it depends on the agent, on Meta, or on a model behaving. If priorities change in month three, or the agent proves harder than expected, you are not negotiating from zero. You are negotiating from a system that already replaced the filing cabinets.

Everything after M2 is upside built on ground you already own. Sequence it that way deliberately, and say so at the week-1 demo.
