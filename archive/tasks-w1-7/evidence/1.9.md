## The structural call: a composite foreign key, not a trigger

R2 says a Unit is a Space of kind `UNIT` and shares its key. Three ways to hold that: an application
rule, a trigger, or a key. It is a key.

```sql
-- space
UNIQUE (space_id, space_kind)

-- unit
space_kind text NOT NULL DEFAULT 'UNIT' CHECK (space_kind = 'UNIT'),
FOREIGN KEY (unit_id, space_kind) REFERENCES space (space_id, space_kind)
```

One key buys three guarantees, and the third is the one that decided it:

1. a Unit with no Space is rejected — the acceptance bar;
2. a Unit on a `COMMON`, `TECHNICAL`, `EXTERIOR`, `PARKING` or `STORAGE` space is rejected, which is
   what makes foundation rule 6 (`UNIT` is the only kind that can ever be the tenant's) true at the
   bottom rather than asserted at the top;
3. **a space cannot stop being a `UNIT` while its unit exists.** An insert trigger would not have
   caught that, and it is the shape the rule actually dies in — nobody creates an apartment on a
   lobby, somebody re-kinds a space a year later.

The cost is one constant column, pinned to one value by a CHECK. It carries no fact and nothing
writes it: the `DEFAULT` is the whole of its content, which is why `tests/policy/fixtures.ts` still
names the workbook's columns and no others.

Workbook decision D3 gets the same mechanism — `parking_space_id` may only point at a `PARKING`
space, `storage_space_id` at a `STORAGE` one. `MATCH SIMPLE` leaves the key unenforced while the id
is null, and unassigned is the ordinary state, so nullability and the constraint do not fight.

## The workbook is read as a specification, and a test says so

`src/estate/schema.test.ts` asserts the exact column set of each of the four tables against the
FIELDS sheet. A migration that drifts from `docs/model/` is red, rather than discovered by someone
comparing two files by eye. The three constant discriminators are named in that list with a comment
saying they are enforcement and not facts — an unexplained extra column is exactly what the parity
test exists to catch, including one of ours.

Absence is asserted the same way. `unit_count`, `occupancy` and `current_tenant` are queried out of
`information_schema.columns` and must return no rows. The grep guard already fails the build on the
string `current_tenant` in a migration file; this checks the schema that is actually deployed rather
than the file that was meant to create it, and it covers the other two, which no guard watches.

## What was deliberately not built

**No natural key, and no uniqueness beyond the primary keys.** `tasks/roadmap.md`'s 1.9 entry said
"natural keys do the work: `address_key` for a building". The FIELDS sheet specifies no such column,
and the real need behind it is an importer that can be run twice — which is slice 1.11's, and 1.11 is
also the first slice with real Shoham addresses in front of it to say what the key should be. Adding
it now would have pinned a guess at the cost of a migration; adding it there costs the same
migration against real data. Carried into 1.11's entry in both plan files.

The local proof that this is a live question and not a hypothetical: `seedUnit` in the policy
fixtures creates a *new* building row per call, all with the address `Rakefet 12, Shoham`. A unique
constraint on the address would have failed the isolation cases today. What the right key is, is
genuinely open.

**No timestamp columns.** E1–E4 have none in the workbook, and SPEC.md bans `DEFAULT now()`; a
`created_at` written by anything but the injected clock is a second source of truth no test can see.
When estate needs one it arrives with the command that writes it.

**Enums are `text` + `CHECK`, not Postgres enum types**, following `0002_kernel_durability.sql`. Both
cost a migration to widen. A CHECK is visible in `\d`, greppable in the migration, and does not
require an `ALTER TYPE` outside a transaction.

## Observations, which are not assertions

**`UNIQUE (space_id, space_kind)` duplicates part of the primary key's index.** It is not redundant —
a foreign key may only reference a UNIQUE constraint, so it is the target R2 and D3 need — but it is
a second index over a column already covered, and at 1,500 units it costs nothing worth measuring.
Noted because the reflex on reading it is to delete it, and deleting it breaks three constraints.

**Two of the twelve cases in the first suite are descriptive, not defensive.** "An apartment is a
space with a unit, a lobby is a space with none" and "an unassigned bay is the ordinary case" passed
against a schema with no R2 in it. They stay, because they document the shape the workbook describes
— but they are not what proves the bar, and the red run is the only thing that separates the two
kinds of case.
